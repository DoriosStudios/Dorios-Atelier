# Painting

Paint Buckets, Paintbrushes and Paint Rollers use the creator assets imported by `tools/import-atelier-assets.py`. There are 38 paint colors: Minecraft's 16 colors and Atelier's 22 extra concrete and wool colors. Brushes and rollers follow the seven active equipment tiers: Wooden, Stone, Copper, Iron, Golden, Diamond and Netherite. Steel artwork is preserved for future tier support.

Put a filled Paint Bucket in the offhand. The first use of a clean brush or roller, in the air or on any ordinary block, loads that bucket's color onto the tool and ends the interaction without attempting to paint the block. Its bristles or roller pad take that color, while handles and metal parts retain their original colors. The tool's localized lore records the loaded paint. Dipping is free and the loaded color persists when the tool is moved or the world is reopened. A loaded tool can change its paint by being used in the air with another filled bucket. Painting requires a matching bucket in the offhand.

Using the tool on water, flowing water, a water-filled cauldron, or UtilityCraft's Sink washes off its paint instead. Cleaning takes priority over loading, even with a filled bucket in the offhand, and clears the saved color, tinted icon and paint lore without damaging the tool. Empty cauldrons, lava and powder-snow cauldrons are not cleaning targets. An already-clean tool gives a localized message. Using a Paint Bucket without a painting tool in the main hand explains how to equip the two items. Loading, cleaning and bucket guidance are translated into all five supported locales; paired item-use callbacks cannot immediately repaint a washed tool or paint a block during the initial loading interaction.

Use the loaded tool on an eligible block. A brush paints the selected block. A roller paints up to nine exposed, eligible blocks centered on that block, in the plane of the clicked face. North/South uses the X/Y plane, East/West uses Y/Z, and Up/Down uses X/Z. Painting a wall never wraps onto the adjacent floor or around a corner. Block orientation, stair facing, vertical half and other shared permutation states are retained.

The destination keeps its resolved dye-color state: legacy vanilla `color` states and namespaced `:color` states are not copied from the source. Copying that state would restore the old color. The directly clicked center is already confirmed by the interaction event and is not rejected because a partial neighboring block, such as a snow layer, occupies part of its face. For the roller's additional cells, an occupied neighboring cell triggers a visibility ray to the selected face instead of automatically excluding the block. Air and liquids do not require a visibility ray.

Every roller stroke independently resolves all nine positions, even when the center has no matching color or is already painted. Each position uses its own original color, material and shape. The replacements are prepared before applying any block changes, so updates from an earlier replacement cannot alter how later destinations are chosen. An unavailable or disappearing block does not cancel the remaining area. These checks run only during an interaction, with at most eight additional visibility rays when neighboring cells are occupied.

Painting has no material whitelist. Color-prefixed blocks of any material can be painted when the matching destination is registered in the world, including blocks from other add-ons. For example, `other:white_marble` can become `other:red_marble` without adding a marble rule. Concrete powder also follows this resolution. A missing color, finish or shape is skipped without consuming durability. Painting does not generate new block definitions or change the material portion of an identifier.

A successful painting action consumes **one bucket durability point and one tool durability point**, regardless of how many of the nine roller cells changed. Dipping, ineligible blocks, unavailable colors, covered faces and blocks already in the selected color cost nothing. Buckets provide 256 painting actions and become an Empty Paint Bucket when depleted. Tool durability follows the existing tiers. Creative mode does not consume durability; Adventure and Spectator cannot paint.

Craft an Empty Paint Bucket with three Iron Ingots and two Sticks. Combine it with a Water Bucket and a matching vanilla or Atelier Dye to fill it. Craft a Paintbrush with one White Wool, one tier material and one Stick; a Paint Roller uses three White Wool, one tier material and one Stick. Filling a partially used colored bucket through another recipe is intentionally unavailable.

`content/painting.json` owns durability, tier materials and the vanilla color palette; extra colors come from `content/concrete-colors.json`. `npm run content:painting:apply` derives the tint masks and generates items, recipes, the Painting creative group, all five localizations and the RGB palette in `BP/scripts/paintingData.js`. Regenerate when changing the palette or painting items; new compatible blocks need no painting-data regeneration. `npm run content:painting:check` verifies reproducibility. Runtime handlers work only when a tool is used; they do not scan players or blocks on an interval.

## Adaptive block resolution

`BP/scripts/paintingResolver.js` recognizes the longest matching color prefix, including compound colors such as `light_blue` and `light_gray`, then replaces that prefix while retaining the material, finish and shape. There are no `concrete`, `wool`, `glass` or other material-name filters. An uncolored base can also resolve to a registered color-prefixed counterpart; vanilla glass and terracotta retain their naming exceptions. Before changing a block, it checks that the destination exists with `BlockTypes.get`. It preserves shared permutation states when applying the replacement. An already-colored center can anchor a roller stroke that changes its neighbors.

The original namespace has priority. Minecraft and Atelier can exchange matching destinations, allowing vanilla White Wool to become Atelier Navy Wool and back. Other add-ons' compatible color-prefixed blocks resolve within their own namespace, so an unavailable color never replaces their block with another add-on's material. Uncolored vanilla glass, glass panes and terracotta have a few family-name exceptions; legacy `silver`/`lima` colors and `clear_glass`/`clean_glass` spellings are also recognized. No block-by-block destination list is maintained.

Both successful and missing destinations are cached by source identifier and requested color, with at most 4,096 entries. New compatible finishes and shapes are discovered from their names and the live registry, including matching blocks supplied by other add-ons. This relies on consistent names; unrelated naming schemes need a small naming rule rather than an exhaustive block table.

## Native color rendering

Tools use native `minecraft:dyeable` and separate `default`/`dyed` icon entries. Dipping sets `ItemDyeableComponent.color` to the loaded paint's normalized RGB. Derived TGA icons mark the paint region with alpha 255, fixed visible hardware with alpha 3, and the background with alpha 0. The masks in `content/paint-texture-masks.json` select only the bristles or roller pad. Original PNGs remain unchanged and provide the unloaded icon. See Microsoft's [custom item tutorial](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/minecraft/creator/documents/addcustomitems?view=minecraft-bedrock-stable) and [ItemDyeableComponent reference](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/minecraft/creator/scriptapi/minecraft/server/itemdyeablecomponent?view=minecraft-bedrock-stable).

Each of the 38 colored Paint Buckets uses its existing creator PNG directly through a single item-atlas texture path. Buckets do not use layered atlas composition or `tint_color`. `tools/prepare-paint-textures.py` generates only the 14 selectively dyeable tool icons; the obsolete bucket base/paint layers were removed. Inventory, held-item and Vibrant Visuals rendering still require in-game verification.
